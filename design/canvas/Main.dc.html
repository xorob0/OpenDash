openDash
Design system · tokens 0.9.2 · dash, second screens and plugin
The brand is achromatic. Colour is reserved for meaning.
Everything is layered. A colour starts as a step on a palette scale, becomes a meaning (danger, best, a surface, a text role) with a primary, a secondary and a tint shade, and is finally named for the one place it is used: the delta when slower, the last lap when it equals the session best, the blue flag. Nothing on any surface reads a palette step directly, and nothing on the dash face reads anything but a purpose token.
The parts are layered the same way. Elements (a label, a numeral, a segment) make components (a readout, a rev bar, one component per flag); components make cards, which are the changeable modules the plugin drops into slots; and a layout is a screen size that fixes the hero and declares how many slots it has. The dash face, the companion and the pit wall carry no identity colour at all, since SimHub renders all three; the plugin and the documentation are where openDash cyan lives.

Illustration · the drawn objects
illustration.tyre
Tread left fills the carcass from the bottom, in three columns because that is what iRacing reports: inner, middle, outer. The fill takes the temperature band, so a nominal tyre is white, a cold one blue and a hot one red, and a tyre worn on its inner shoulder is visible as a shape before any number is read. The tick marks a wheel the crew will change.
illustration.disc
A brake disc is round, so the drawing is round. This is not an exception to rule 7 but the line it draws: an illustration takes the shape of the thing it depicts, and chrome stays square.

One drawing serves damage and the pit order: panels take a state colour when they are hurt, wheels take a tick when the crew will change them.

09
Layout · the zones, and what a driver changes with a thumb
One could think a face is a grid of cards a user arranges once, and two passes of this canvas assumed exactly that: first twelve equal slots, then cells a card could span. In reality a driver does not configure a dashboard, they change it mid-stint with a thumb. The face is therefore a small number of named zones, each holding one page at a time from its own catalogue and each bound to a wheel button, which is how every mature package in this field works and why their screens do not look interchangeable. What a bigger screen buys is not more cards but more in each zone: zone C lists seven drivers at 850 by 480 and eighteen at 1280 by 720, and a page drops its secondary rows before it shrinks its numerals.

Type · one family, two widths
Barlow Condensed · every numeral, on every surface

Weight 600 to 700. The digits are proportional, 0.284 em for the 1 against 0.484 for the 4 in Bold, and the tabular alternates the font carries under tnum cannot be requested by SimHub. Values are drawn in monospace cells instead, 0.47 em per character in SemiBold and 0.49 in Bold, which is within a percent of the font's own tabular advance. The cell was cut for digits, so a value may not contain # % & @ M W m w, whose ink overruns it. Tracking −0.01em applies in the plugin and the documentation only, since SimHub renders everything else.

12
Rules · the part that survives a redesign
1
Nominal is achromatic. A value at rest is white or grey. It takes a colour only when its meaning changes: faster, slower, caution, alarm, flag, session best. Colour is always an event, never decoration.
2
Three layers, always. Palette steps are never read directly. A surface reads semantic tokens; the dash face reads purpose tokens only. A new card names what it means, and the meaning already has a shade.
3
Cyan is identity, and it stays off the face. Wordmark, one primary action per panel, focus ring, links, progress. On the dash, the pit wall and the companion every chroma is a purpose token.
4
Numerals are always Barlow Condensed weight 600 or 700, in monospace cells under rule 19: lap times on the dash, table rows on the pit wall, version numbers in the plugin. Text that is not a number is Barlow.
5
One label idiom, two renderers. Barlow 500, uppercase, text.label, 4px above its value. 15px on the dash and 13px on the second screens with no tracking, since SimHub has none; 12px tracked 0.14em in the plugin.
6
Hierarchy comes from size, not weight or colour. The dash runs 17:1 from gear to label. The plugin runs just over 2:1 from wordmark to label and never adds bold to make something important.
7
Hard edges, except where something is drawn. Radius 0 on the dash, with the shift segment as the one exception at 2, and the illustrations, which take their objects' shapes under rule 14. Radius 2 also on plugin controls and on the segment mark beside the wordmark, and nowhere else. A status dot is a 6px square.
8
Zones are separated by 1px rules, never boxes. Slots are separated by rules; the only filled rectangle is a list row, an input or a pop-up, in surface.zone. Nothing floats, nothing casts a shadow.
9
The hero never moves; everything else is a card. Gear, speed, rev bar, flag and pit limiter are fixed per layout. Every other value is a card that fits every slot of its layout, chosen from the plugin. A card that needs more room is two cards.
10
One component per flag and per alert. Twenty-five alerts share one band anatomy, solid, outlined or patterned, and differ in colour, label, priority and duration. One shows at a time; none is drawn when none is active; a round face uses its ring instead of a band.
11
Motion only carries meaning. The rev bar flashes at 8 Hz on redline, a flag band at 2 Hz, a pop-up lasts 3 s. The plugin animates one thing, a progress bar. Nothing eases in, nothing bounces.
12
Icons are drawn, not typed. 16px in controls, 20px alone, 1.5px stroke, square caps and mitre joins, one colour from the text scale. Never emoji, never a filled glyph next to a stroked one.
13
A rule separates; almost nothing is filled. The only filled rectangles are a list row, an input and a pop-up. A drawing needs no tray under it and a group of values needs no box: the rule between zones is the whole boundary. The one fill that is not a rectangle of content is the well, the recessed ground behind the rev bar, which is depth rather than a box.
14
Draw only what a shape says better than a number. A tyre has shoulders and three tread bands, a car has a nose and four corners, a disc is round: those carry something a number does not. Fuel is a quantity, so fuel is a number. Colour inside a drawing is still a purpose token, and a nominal tyre is white.
15
A zone shows one page, and a driver changes it with a thumb. A face is a rev bar, a bar of settled values, zones A, B and C across the body, and band D. A page fills the zone it is given and drops its secondary rows before it shrinks its numerals, so a bigger screen shows more in each zone rather than more cards of the same size.
16
A telltale is the standard's, not ours. ISO 2575 fixes the pictogram and the colour, and drivers already know both. They are taken unmodified from Material Design Icons under the Apache Licence 2.0, they are filled where a user-interface icon is stroked, and an unlit one is drawn dim rather than hidden.
17
A page answers to the shape of its zone. Not to its width alone. A long shallow zone takes one rank, a tall narrow one stacks its values and sizes them to the height, and a page sheds its secondary rows before it shrinks its numerals. Nothing is ever scaled down.
18
A drawing is cut from its box. Never placed in it at a fixed size. A tyre is as tall as the readings beside it, a car is capped at a third of the zone width however tall the box is, and the traces take what is left. A drawing that keeps one size while the numbers around it grow ends up looking like a stamp on a poster.
19
A value is laid in cells, and only what fits a cell may be drawn. Barlow Condensed's digits are proportional and SimHub cannot ask the font for its tabular ones, so every value is drawn with SimHub's monospace cells. The cell was cut for digits, and WPF clips a glyph that overruns it: # % & @ M W m w are therefore never part of a value. A car number is drawn bare and its hash belongs to the label; a class beside a number is a proportional label and a monospaced value, not one string.
13
What changed in tokens 0.9.2, and what is still open
tokens.json is at 0.9.2: font.cell records the monospace cells a value is drawn in, and rule 19 says what may go in one. The question this closes had been open since the first token file: the digits are proportional, the tabular alternates the font carries are unreachable from SimHub, and the cells were measured to hold the widest ink a value can draw. At 0.9.1, every drawing became cut from its box rather than placed in it at a fixed size, which is rule 18. At 0.9.0 came the shape model above: a page is laid out for the shape of its zone rather than its width, and every page now has a version for every shape a real face gives it. At 0.8.0, the telltales in section 08 and the icon library behind them. At 0.7.0, The drawings lost their fills and the fuel tank was dropped, because a drawing on the face needs no tray under it and a quantity is a number; band D became a centred rank with a corner block at each end; and a zone narrow enough for one column centres what is in it. At 0.6.0 the slot and cell models were replaced by the zone model in section 08: a bar of settled values, zones A, B and C, band D, and a catalogue and a button per zone. card.span stays only as a record of the pass it supersedes. At 0.4.0, and worth a look: purpose.licence, the ordered weight ramp above; purpose.rating; purpose.block, which finally says when a fill is allowed and puts the rev bar in the well the very first token file named; purpose.illustration; and metrics for block, badge, flagIcon and each drawn object. The hero also lost the speed it should not have had: the built dashboard holds the gear alone with its neighbours ghosted either side, and speed is card 12 in slot 1. Earlier, at 0.3.0: the palette layer with 100 and 300 steps (new hex values, off the face); the semantic layer with primary, secondary and tint per meaning; the purpose layer; size.gearSm 180; the ui ramp with numeralLg 24; control tokens; card rungs and padding; indicator sizes for the flag band, the flag ring, the pit limiter and the pop-up; the screen list with a slot count and grid per size, including the companion and pit wall sizes; and, with the alert catalogue, purpose.flag.red, orange and debris, purpose.alert for penalties, incidents, power warnings and push to pass, purpose.trend, purpose.class for the chips, and durations for alerts, notifications and the lap review.
Still open: the tyre temperature thresholds behind purpose.tyre.cold and hot, left as [T_COLD] and [T_HOT] until validated in iRacing; whether weight 700 blooms on a lit panel at label size, which only the physical DDU can answer; and the exact SimHub style values (SHButtonPrimary, SHToggleButton, section titles) that the plugin panel inherits, which are lifted from SimHub's resource dictionaries at build time rather than drawn here.
